## Emergency Response Priorities

A commercial water emergency can affect electrical systems, mechanical equipment, inventory, tenant operations and building access.

The first priorities are protecting people, identifying the source, preventing additional water intrusion and documenting affected areas. Restoration planning can then move into extraction, moisture mapping, controlled drying, cleaning, repairs and continued monitoring.

Never enter an area with standing water near electrical equipment, visible structural instability, contaminated water or other unsafe conditions.

## Structural Drying and Moisture Detection

Visible water is only part of a commercial loss. Moisture can remain inside wall cavities, insulation, flooring systems, ceiling assemblies and structural materials.

Professional moisture detection helps define the affected area. A documented drying plan may include extraction equipment, air movers, commercial dehumidifiers, containment systems and ongoing moisture measurements.

## Business Continuity

Commercial restoration is not only about drying a building. The response must also consider employees, tenants, customers, inventory, equipment and daily operations.

An effective recovery plan may divide the property into work zones, protect unaffected areas and coordinate restoration activities with facility personnel, plumbers, electricians, engineers and general contractors.

## Common Causes of Commercial Water Damage

- Plumbing and supply-line failures
- Burst or frozen pipes
- Roof and drainage failures
- Fire-suppression system discharge
- HVAC and mechanical-system leaks
- Appliance and equipment failures
- Sewer backups
- Stormwater intrusion
- Groundwater and exterior drainage problems
- Construction-related water intrusion
