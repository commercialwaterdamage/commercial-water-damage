# Commercial Water Damage Emergency Response Checklist

Use this checklist during the first stage of a commercial water-loss event. Building safety always comes before cleanup, documentation or normal business operations.

For professional guidance, visit [Commercial Water Damage](https://commercialwaterdamage.co/).

## 1. Protect People

- Keep employees, tenants, customers and visitors away from affected areas.
- Call emergency services when there is an immediate threat to life or safety.
- Do not enter areas with standing water near electrical equipment.
- Avoid rooms with sagging ceilings, structural damage or falling debris.
- Treat sewage, floodwater and unknown water sources as potentially contaminated.
- Establish a safe boundary around the affected portion of the property.

## 2. Stop the Water Source When Safe

- Identify the likely source of the water.
- Shut off the appropriate water supply only when it can be reached safely.
- Contact the building engineer, plumber or facility manager.
- Inspect plumbing, HVAC, roofing, drainage and fire-suppression systems.
- Do not disable required fire-protection systems without qualified authorization.
- Record when the source was discovered and controlled.

## 3. Notify the Response Team

Contact the appropriate people as quickly as possible:

- Qualified commercial water-damage professional
- Property owner or authorized decision-maker
- Facility manager and building engineer
- Security or risk-management personnel
- Tenant representatives when applicable
- Insurance carrier according to reporting requirements
- Contractors needed for plumbing, electrical, roofing or structural hazards

Identify who is authorized to approve emergency work and building access.

## 4. Document Initial Conditions

Take photographs and video only from safe areas.

Document:

- Apparent source of the water
- Standing water and visibly affected materials
- Damaged inventory, furniture and equipment
- Affected building systems
- Floors, rooms, suites and operational areas involved
- Date and approximate time the water was discovered
- Security-camera footage that may show the incident
- Calls, decisions, approvals and site-access activity

Avoid moving damaged property until initial documentation is complete unless movement is necessary for safety.

## 5. Protect Building Operations

- Determine which areas must be closed or restricted.
- Establish safe employee and tenant access routes.
- Protect unaffected rooms from spreading moisture.
- Relocate vulnerable inventory, records and equipment when safe.
- Coordinate shutdowns involving electrical, mechanical or fire-protection systems.
- Notify occupants about closures without making unverified safety claims.
- Identify priority areas needed to maintain essential operations.

## 6. Begin Water Extraction

Standing water should be removed by qualified personnel using equipment appropriate for the size and type of loss.

Before extraction begins:

- Confirm electrical conditions are safe.
- Identify the water-contamination category.
- Inspect floors below and rooms adjoining the affected area.
- Protect drains and unaffected spaces.
- Document conditions before removing damaged materials.
- Confirm that extraction will not spread contaminated water.

## 7. Inspect for Concealed Moisture

Visible water rarely represents the complete affected area.

Inspect:

- Wall cavities
- Insulation
- Flooring and subfloor systems
- Suspended ceilings
- Structural framing
- Concrete and masonry
- Mechanical rooms
- Electrical and communication spaces
- Elevator pits
- Cabinets and built-in fixtures

Commercial moisture meters, thermal-imaging cameras and controlled inspection openings may be required to define the full loss.

## 8. Establish Structural Drying

A commercial drying plan may include:

- Water-extraction equipment
- Air movers
- Commercial dehumidifiers
- Temperature management
- Air filtration
- Containment barriers
- Controlled demolition
- Documented moisture-monitoring locations
- Daily equipment and drying records

Equipment placement should reflect the building layout, affected materials, occupancy and available electrical capacity.

## 9. Coordinate Repairs

Emergency mitigation and permanent repairs should be treated as connected stages.

Potential work may involve:

- Plumbing repairs
- Electrical inspection
- HVAC evaluation
- Roofing and drainage corrections
- Drywall and insulation replacement
- Flooring removal and installation
- Structural repairs
- Storefront glass and exterior-envelope repairs
- Tenant-improvement reconstruction
- Painting and finish restoration

Do not begin reconstruction until acceptable moisture conditions have been verified.

## 10. Maintain a Recovery Record

Keep one organized project file containing:

- Photographs and videos
- Moisture readings
- Equipment logs
- Inspection reports
- Contractor information
- Repair estimates
- Insurance communications
- Access and safety decisions
- Completed-work dates
- Final drying verification
- Repair and closeout documentation

## Immediate Response Summary

1. Protect occupants.
2. Restrict unsafe areas.
3. Identify and control the source.
4. Notify the response team.
5. Document initial conditions.
6. Extract standing water.
7. Map visible and concealed moisture.
8. Establish structural drying.
9. Coordinate permanent repairs.
10. Verify and document final conditions.

## Safety Notice

This checklist provides general educational information. Water-damaged buildings may contain electrical, structural, environmental or biological hazards. Contact emergency personnel and properly licensed professionals whenever conditions may be unsafe.
