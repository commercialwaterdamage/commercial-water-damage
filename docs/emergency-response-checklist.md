Commercial Water Damage Emergency Response Checklist
Use this checklist during the first stage of a commercial water-loss event. Building safety always comes before cleanup, documentation or business operations.

1. Protect People
 Keep employees, tenants, customers and visitors away from affected areas.
 Call emergency services when there is an immediate threat to life or safety.
 Do not enter areas with standing water near electrical equipment.
 Avoid rooms with sagging ceilings, damaged structural materials or falling debris.
 Treat sewage, floodwater and unknown water sources as potentially contaminated.
 Establish a safe boundary around the affected portion of the property.
2. Stop the Water Source When Safe
 Identify the likely source of the water.
 Shut off the appropriate water supply only if it can be reached safely.
 Contact the building engineer, plumber or facility manager.
 Check fire-suppression, plumbing, HVAC, roofing and drainage systems.
 Do not disable required fire-protection systems without qualified authorization.
 Record when the source was discovered and controlled.
3. Notify the Response Team
 Contact a qualified commercial water-damage professional.
 Notify the property owner or designated decision-maker.
 Alert the facility manager and building engineer.
 Contact security, risk management and tenant representatives when applicable.
 Notify the insurance carrier according to the property’s reporting requirements.
 Identify the person authorized to approve emergency work and building access.
4. Document Initial Conditions
Take photographs or video only from safe areas.

 Record the apparent source of the loss.
 Photograph standing water and visibly affected materials.
 Document damaged inventory, furniture, equipment and building systems.
 Record the date and approximate time the water was discovered.
 Note which floors, rooms, suites and operational areas are affected.
 Preserve security-camera footage when it may show the incident.
 Maintain a written log of calls, decisions and site access.
5. Protect Building Operations
 Determine which areas must close immediately.
 Redirect employees, tenants and visitors away from restoration zones.
 Protect unaffected inventory, records and equipment when safe.
 Identify critical operations that require temporary relocation.
 Coordinate access for restoration crews, plumbers and building professionals.
 Communicate verified information without speculating about reopening times.
6. Prepare for Water Extraction and Drying
Commercial water can move beneath flooring, behind walls and through ceiling assemblies.

 Identify all visibly affected areas.
 Request moisture inspection beyond the visible water line.
 Check adjoining rooms, lower floors and concealed building cavities.
 Confirm whether controlled demolition may be required.
 Establish equipment locations and safe electrical access.
 Separate restoration zones from occupied areas when appropriate.
 Begin a documented structural-drying plan.
7. Track the Recovery
 Keep daily moisture readings and drying records.
 Document equipment placement and changes.
 Record removed materials and retained building components.
 Coordinate plumbing, electrical, HVAC and construction work.
 Update tenants and operational teams at agreed intervals.
 Confirm drying goals before repairs and reconstruction begin.
 Store photographs, invoices, reports and communications together.
Information to Have Ready
When contacting a commercial restoration provider, prepare:

Property address
Primary contact name and callback number
Type and source of water emergency
Approximate affected area
Property type and occupancy
Known safety hazards
Floors, rooms or suites affected
Current building-access instructions
Photos or video when safely available

Safety Notice
This checklist provides general information and does not replace emergency services, site-specific safety procedures or guidance from qualified professionals. Do not enter an area that may contain electrical, structural, environmental, chemical or biological hazards.
